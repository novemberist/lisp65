# v1.2.6 editor stall — JTAG one-key method review

Date: 2026-07-31

Candidate: Link 83, non-promotable

Disposition: **owner decision 2026-07-31 — Option 1 commissioned, Option 2
prepared as fallback.** The per-key JTAG classification is retired.

Commission: rebuild the state with ordinary input; verify every
postcondition from live buffer memory (never the status line, never a
per-key breakpoint); RUN/STOP is proven by its product postcondition (a
recovered REPL). The device is stopped **once**, when a buffer-fill wait
actually fails, and the queue/`gc_runs`/PC peeks execute against the live
hang. **One contact plus one reserve.** If setup fails again or the hang
does not reproduce under this method, no further contact follows — the
Option-2 pre-staged diagnostic medium (with its equivalence proof
obligation) becomes the next commission instead. The transport-arrival
question is closed by contacts 8–10 and is not re-proven per key.

## Result

The complete precomputed expectation table was bound before contact 10:
318 ordered `(sequence, phase, payload, expected PETSCII)` rows, derived from
the existing disk-directory fold authority.  Five table mutations and seven
runner mutations were rejected; the host dry-run executed 333 checks and sent
zero device commands.

Contact 10 then passed all 41 ordinary typed keys preceding the scratch-editor
abort.  It stopped on row 42, the `~C`/RUN/STOP exception.  The breakpoint was
set at `$E00B`; this core reported the stopped CPU at `$E017`, with A=`$00` and
`C2K_BREAK_PENDING` already clear.  The runner expected the previously modeled
post-instruction stop at `$E00E` with nonzero A, so it rejected the event.

This is an attributable **tooling First Red**.  It does not reproduce or
classify the editor product stall, and outcomes (a), (b), and (c) remain
unmeasured.  The breakpoint was cleared and the CPU resumed after the receipt
was written.  No product byte, link, or diagnostic patch changed.

## Owner alternatives

No option below is authorized by this review.

1. **Abandon per-key JTAG classification; stop once on the product event
   (recommended for another attempt).** Reconstruct the state with ordinary
   input, verify each postcondition from live buffer memory, and take the
   queue/GC/PC peeks only when a buffer-fill wait actually fails.  RUN/STOP is
   then proven by its product postcondition (recovered REPL), not forced into a
   typed-queue breakpoint model.  This removes the two classifier assumptions
   that consumed contacts 8–10, at the cost of not proving arrival for every
   successful setup key.
2. **Build a non-promotable pre-staged diagnostic identity.** Put the exact
   scratch/helper/`measure3` state on the diagnostic medium so the device starts
   immediately before the 56-key workload.  This removes 262 setup keystrokes
   and RUN/STOP from the transport, but requires a separately reviewed fixture
   build and a proof that the pre-staged state is equivalent.
3. **Use a product-side one-shot witness.** A diagnostic-only marker at GC
   entry/exit or the render/persist seam would turn the next stall into one
   passive read rather than 318 JTAG stop/resume cycles.  It costs diagnostic
   product bytes and therefore needs a new Class-C commission and fresh map.
4. **Re-park the editor stall.** The renderer allocation improvement remains
   real (24.025 frames/key for the first 40 versus 78 before), but v1.2.6 stays
   closed and the deterministic key-56 target symptom remains documented.

The cheapest method correction is option 1; the strongest isolation is option
2.  After ten contacts, repeating the current one-key method is not an option.
