# C2.2 historical gate inheritance audit

Date: 2026-07-21

The Link-33 hardware First Red exposed a process failure in addition to the
llvm-mos code-generation defect: the target-stable CRC decrement gate lived
inside `workbench-overlay-control-audit`, so it silently stopped guarding the
product when the Workbench boot-overlay carrier retired.

The machine-readable inventory is
`config/c2-historical-gate-inheritance.json`. It covers the host-side product
and overlay gates attached to that retired carrier. Every property has one of
four explicit outcomes: migrated into the central C2 product-link closure,
superseded by named C2 gates, retired with a reason because its architecture no
longer exists, or retained as hardware-only because a structural link cannot
prove it.

Two live properties were orphaned and are now migrated into every C2 product
link:

- `c2_crc_codegen_gate.py` pins the resident CRC section and its sole catalog
  call, forbids `DEW`, requires two distinct byte `DEC` operands and pointer
  progress, and rejects return of the old duplicate CRC helper.
- `f011_mount_window.py` remeasures the guarded D68F-to-D081 instruction window
  on the final linked C2 ELF.

The old overlay reproducibility check is deliberately not restored. WPLTO is
not a claimed pure function across invocations; the active contract instead
pins the single built identity, makes its evidence read-only, and restricts all
post-link mutation to the enumerated 34-byte publish-last domain.

Hardware stack/readback behavior remains in the presmoke and full hardware
protocol. Moving it into a structural receipt would be a false claim rather
than a migration.
