Choose whether to park the `defstruct` library freight now, or commission one

## What is proven

- The stale compiler-carrier defect is fixed on hardware:
  `(intern "abc")` returns `abc`.
- The Link-75-bound library-media successor was uploaded and read back
  byte-identically. Both L65S envelopes carry product build ID `0x911d21fe`

  CLEAR before execution.

- An immediate second `(require 'defstruct)` returned `t`; the resolver's
  generations-bound idempotence path therefore ran on hardware.
- The already commissioned DMA series completed before this retry:
  homogeneous and mixed jobs were stable across all three batches. Its
  classification is `no-reproduction-no-fix`.
- `(defstruct point x y)` then entered the product's red fail-closed frame
  without a result or trailing prompt.

The harness now treats the red frame as a terminal First Red. The former
180-second blind polling behavior is removed.

## What is not proven

- No `defstruct`-generated constructor, accessor, predicate or place mutation
  has run on hardware.
- The First Red does not identify which operation inside the multi-definition
  expansion entered `c2_kernal_fail_closed`.
- The clean DMA result does not prove a cause elsewhere; it only forbids
  reopening the completed DMA question without a new commission.
- No product or library correction is authorized by the observation alone.

## Option A — park `defstruct` (recommended)

Remove `defstruct` from the active 1.2.x freight claim and keep the complete
First-Red package as its restart point. Do not change the released v1.2.0
product or claim the unfinished library surface.

Consequence: the published system and the independently proven `require`

Consequence: `defstruct` remains active freight, but it receives a new
explicit budget rather than inheriting the exhausted carrier/DMA inquiry.

## Owner decision (2026-07-28): **Option A — park `defstruct`**

### Restart package — read this first when the era resumes

Three items are recorded here so the restart does not begin at zero. None of
them is authorized work; they are the queue.

**R-1. Complete interrupt inventory: three internal mask gaps.**
The later exact-core inventory supersedes the original exemplary list. F011/SD
has **no CPU-interrupt edge** on the tested core, so mounted D81 media cannot
directly produce the source-less IRQ previously proposed here. Buffered-UART
also drives its nominal IRQ output permanently inactive. The real internal
gaps are Ethernet (RX/TX), Auto-IEC (RX/ready/timeout), and the CPU-internal
four-channel Audio-DMA IRQ. The proposed ownership contract masks and
acknowledges those three families before `CLI`; interrupt-generating
cartridges are an explicit external profile exclusion. Full authority:
`config/c2-interrupt-ownership-policy.json` and
`c2.2-interrupt-ownership-halt2.md`.

Audio-DMA does not need to be used by the product for the gap to matter:
firmware or the hypervisor can leave `$D713` interrupt enables armed before
ownership. This is the first named mechanism that could explain *this*
`defstruct` red frame without product participation, but causation is
unproven. Closing the ownership gap remains independently justified
robustness work, not an implicit unpark of the freight.

**R-2. `require` latency: measured 12 s first / 9 s repeat; parser dominates.**
The later valid, full-reset hardware authority supersedes the earlier coarse
~30-second observation. The idempotent second call loads nothing (C2D and
Bank 2 remain byte-identical), yet the host runner measures 134,788 VM
instructions and 384 Prim-67 reads — 98.55% and 96.24% of the first call.
The parser consumes 105,974 instructions (about 79%); `%l65i-crc-bits` alone
consumes 83,226. The measured defect is therefore identity being checked
*after* reparsing the complete description, not a demonstrated GC or
one-byte-seam bottleneck.

The earlier **82-frame** number was also misread: Link 57 measured one
workload containing two collections and established no isolated collection
time. The valid isolated observations are **88 frames** (Link 66) and
**89 frames** (final G5), each with one collection and 96 contract root-block
reads. Existing evidence has no per-phase timestamps, so no mark/sweep/root
share is claimed.

Owner disposition on restart: commission identity-before-parser with a
512-byte Bank-2 / zero-resident budget and require at least 90% reductions in
both repeat steps and Prim-67 reads. The host-qualified candidate measures
2,750 steps and 26 reads (−97.96% / −93.23%). GC mechanism work remains
deferred until the existing phase 17–20 seams are measured on hardware.

**R-3. Method note.** During the carrier hunt the reviewer's mechanism
hypotheses were refuted five times by static attribution. Order of work on
restart: read and attribute first, hypothesize second.

### Final bounded reopening (2026-07-29): terminally closed

Link 78 reproduced the same red fail-closed frame after the strict interrupt
ownership masks were active. The owner commissioned one last terminal
blackbox with a hard budget of one WPLTO, one successor link and one hardware
run.

The candidate preserved every returning IRQ byte, the generic fail-closed
body and the 16-byte window state geometry; its 19 mutations and linked
normal-path comparison passed. The WPLTO nevertheless stopped correctly:
the E000 IRQ handler reached the new 65-byte ordinary-resident capture body
without a fixed facade. A new facade vector requires 3 bytes and the fixed
block has 2. This is an architecture/closure First Red, not a text or E000
capacity failure.

The hard budget forbids another construction. No Link 79 or hardware run was
made, the blackbox candidate is withdrawn, Link 78 remains authoritative,
and `defstruct` is **permanently parked for this era**. Evidence:
`tests/bytecode/dialect-v2/evidence/architecture-blocks/c2.2-v1.2.2-fail-closed-blackbox-terminal-first-red.json`.
